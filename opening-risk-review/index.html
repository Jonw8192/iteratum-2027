The Decision This Review Supports

If the doors opened tomorrow, could the location actually operate?

A location can be physically finished while the technology workstream is still exposed to delays, missing handoffs, untested systems, and unclear ownership.

The Opening Risk Review is not another vendor status meeting. It is an independent assessment of the full technology readiness picture, focused on the operational outcome that matters: whether the location can function when customers and employees arrive.

What Changes After the Review

Risk becomes visible

Leadership can see which technology areas are ready, which need action, and which create a credible opening-day threat.

Ownership becomes explicit

Critical gaps are assigned to a responsible party with due dates and escalation paths instead of sitting between vendors.

The next actions are dated

The review ends with a corrective-action plan built around the actual opening date, not a generic checklist.

What We Review

The complete technology workstream behind an operating location.

We look across the systems, providers, dates, decisions, dependencies, and tests that have to come together before opening day.

Internet & WAN

Carrier order status, install date, circuit details, static IP requirements, backup connectivity, and escalation path.

Network & Wi-Fi

Firewall, switching, access points, cabling dependencies, segmentation, coverage, remote management, and final test readiness.

POS / Booking

Hardware, location setup, platform configuration, printers, integrations, licensing, deployment timing, and operational testing.

Payments

Merchant setup, terminals, gateway configuration, activation status, test transactions, and settlement readiness.

Security & Cameras

Install status, network dependency, remote access, recording, access control, retention, and final provider handoff.

Phones

Numbers, porting, install timing, inbound and outbound calling, voicemail, and location information.

Users & Access

Employee and manager identities, permissions, MFA, email, required applications, and opening-day access.

Devices & SaaS

Computers, tablets, printers, peripherals, required subscriptions, licensing, assignment, and operational readiness.

Vendors & Dependencies

Owners, install dates, lead times, construction dependencies, handoffs, open decisions, escalation routes, and contingencies.

The Review Is End-to-End

A vendor saying “complete” is not the same as a location being ready.

The review looks for the seams between scopes: the camera installer waiting on the network, the POS deployment waiting on payment activation, the network waiting on construction, or the opening team assuming somebody else owns the final test.

Readiness Language

GREEN — Ready

The item is installed, configured, tested, and on track for opening.

YELLOW — Action Required

Risk exists, but there is a credible corrective path with ownership and time remaining.

RED — Opening Risk

A missing dependency, delay, failed test, or unresolved decision creates a credible risk to operations.

Best Fit

Use the review when there is still time to change the outcome.

The strongest fit is a growing multi-location operator with an upcoming opening, several technology vendors, and no single internal owner validating the full readiness picture.

Strong Signals
  • Your opening is roughly 6–16 weeks away

Common Questions

What operators usually want to know before they start.

When should the review happen?

Ideally six to sixteen weeks before opening. The review becomes more valuable when there is still enough time to escalate carrier delays, correct missing dependencies, schedule tests, and approve contingencies.

What does it cost?

The fixed-scope Opening Risk Review is $2,500 per location.

Do you replace our MSP, POS vendor, or IT team?

No. Those providers continue to own their technical scopes. Iteratum evaluates whether the scopes and dependencies come together into an operating location.

Do you install or configure equipment?

No. Cabling, physical installation, specialized configuration, and field service remain with the appropriate technical provider.

What if a vendor is already late?

The review documents the impact, identifies the dependencies affected, establishes the escalation path, and makes the required contingency or decision visible to leadership.

What happens after the review?

You receive the findings and corrective plan. If execution support is warranted, any next engagement is scoped from the actual gaps identified rather than sold by default.
