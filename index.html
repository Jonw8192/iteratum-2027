The Opening-Day Problem

A location can look finished and still be unable to operate.

Construction can be complete and every vendor can report progress. That still does not prove the location can take a payment, get online, print a receipt, record a camera, or give employees the access they need.

Internet is late

The circuit slips, static IP details are missing, or nobody has validated a usable backup plan.

Payments are not ready

The POS may be installed while merchant activation, gateway configuration, terminals, or settlement remain untested.

The network is incomplete

Hardware is onsite, but cabling, Wi-Fi, segmentation, remote management, failover, or final testing is unfinished.

Security is only half done

Cameras may be mounted without recording, remote access, network connectivity, retention validation, or handoff.

Employees cannot work

Managers arrive without working accounts, MFA, application access, POS permissions, email, or required devices.

Ownership disappears between vendors

The GC thinks IT owns it. IT thinks the vendor owns it. The vendor assumes another provider owns the dependency.

The Gap Iteratum Owns

The problem is rarely one vendor. It is everything between them.

Your ISP can finish its scope. Your POS provider can finish its scope. Your camera installer can finish its scope. Iteratum looks across the full technology workstream and asks the only question that matters on opening day: can this location actually operate?

How We Work

Technology readiness is managed weeks before opening — not checked the night before.

The full Iteratum opening method begins roughly sixteen weeks out. For a Risk Review, we use the same framework to identify what should already be complete, what is drifting, and what requires escalation now.

-16

Foundation

Connectivity, technology requirements, vendors, ownership, budget, and major dependencies should be established.

-12

Critical Commitments

Circuits, major vendors, equipment, payments, and implementation activity should be underway.

-8

Risk Exposure

Carrier, network, POS, payment, security, construction, and equipment threats need visible escalation.

-4

Installation

Technology installs, punch lists, defects, and remaining physical dependencies become the focus.

-2

Formal Test

Connectivity, Wi-Fi, POS, payments, printers, cameras, phones, accounts, and applications are validated.

-1

Readiness Decision

Opening-critical items should be green or have an approved contingency, owner, and escalation route.

0

Opening

Technology supports the business instead of becoming the opening-day emergency.

+1

Improve

Incidents, defects, and vendor performance become lessons for the next location.

Common Questions

What operators usually want to know before a review.

When should Iteratum get involved?

Ideally while the opening is still six to sixteen weeks away. The earlier risks are identified, the more options the operator has to correct them.

Does Iteratum replace our MSP or IT provider?

No. Existing providers continue to own their technical scopes. Iteratum evaluates readiness across those scopes and the dependencies between them.

Does Iteratum install equipment?

No. Cabling, physical installation, specialized configuration, and field service remain with the appropriate technical providers.

What happens after the Risk Review?

You receive the findings and corrective plan. If additional help is warranted, the next engagement is scoped around the actual gaps identified rather than selling a larger package by default.
